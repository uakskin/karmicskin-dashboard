01 · FoundationsLocked

The rules the system runs on

A design system is only useful if it removes guesswork. Every colour sits in one of three tiers, and a colour never does a job from another tier. That single idea is what keeps the system clear.

Tier 1

Neutrals build

Porcelain, Carbon and Sand. They make the page — ground, ink and quiet bands. They carry no signal on their own; they are the stage.

Tier 2

The brand signals

One colour — Dark Green. Applied as a field to make a surface feel unmistakably Karmic. Used with restraint, so it always means something.

Tier 3

Accents flag

Brick Red and Amber. Tiny, rare, loud. Red flags an action; amber flags a highlight. Never more than a sliver of any screen.

Five principles
01

Intentional

Every colour, size and space has one job. If you cannot name what it is for, it does not belong on the page.

02

Contrast sets hierarchy

What matters most carries the most contrast. Carbon on Porcelain for what must be read; everything else steps back.

03

Restraint is the point

Few colours, used rarely, hit hardest. A colour spread everywhere stops meaning anything at all.

04

Three tiers, never crossed

Neutrals build, the brand colour signals, accents flag. A colour never borrows another tier's job.

05

One system, every surface

The same rules on the website, the emails, the dashboards and the feed. Consistency is what makes it a system.

03 · Colour in useLocked

Where each colour goes, and where it never does

The palette is the easy part. These are the rules that make it hold together — starting with the one that has caused the most confusion.

Carbon Black + Dark Green · the rule that ends the confusion

Carbon is ink. Green is a field.

The test — works every time
Is it type, an icon, or a line?  →  Carbon Black.
Is it a surface that should feel like Karmic?  →  Dark Green.
Skin, tended
Carbon writes. Every heading, every line of body, every label and icon — on a light ground, it is always Carbon.
Carbon — ink
Skin, tended
Green holds. A panel, a band, a card, the sidebar — a field of green is how a surface says "this is Karmic."
Green — field
Don't

A heading set in green

Green as type loses contrast and spends the brand signal on something small. Green is not an ink.
Do A heading on a green field Want a heading to feel branded? Put it on a green field with Porcelain type — not green letters.

They never compete, because they work on different layers. Carbon is foreground — the detail you read. Green is background — the surface you read against. One is a pen; the other is a wall. Carbon also has the strongest contrast on Porcelain, which is why it earns every word of text. And green is kept to fields precisely so it stays rare — a green surface should feel like a deliberate moment, not wallpaper.

Porcelain · Sand Dune · Dark Green · the only backgrounds

Three backgrounds — and Carbon is never one

Porcelain
The default. Calm and light — almost every surface, almost all of the time.
Sand Dune
A tint. The occasional section band, to give a long page rhythm.
Dark Green
A field. A panel or section meant to feel unmistakably Karmic.

Every surface is one of these three, and only these three. Carbon Black is never a background. It is ink — and it is the product's own colour, the matte-black cap. So Carbon is everywhere in the photography, as the product itself, but the canvas behind the product is always Porcelain, Sand or Green. Black is what you shoot, not what you shoot it on.

Brick Red · #B22222

Red is action, and only action

One red action per screen. Secondary is a green field; ghost is a Carbon outline.

Red carries one message: take this action. The Add to Cart button, the checkout. Never a heading, never decoration, never a text colour. Its power is scarcity — hold it to one thing per screen and the eye goes straight to it.

Amber Honey · #EFA71D

Amber is the glow — a mark, never type

Your skin has a memory. Give it something worth remembering.

A clean, nourished canvas is the foundation of every ritual.

Bestseller   New in   Pregnancy safe

Don't

Amber as a text colour

1.8:1 on Porcelain — far below the 4.5:1 type needs. It washes out.
Do

Amber behind the words

Dark Carbon ink on an amber mark — warm, and fully legible.

Amber works as a mark — an underline, a highlighter wash, a badge, a fill. Dark type sits on it, never in it. When you genuinely need gold on the letters themselves, that is the one job of Deep Ochre — and only at large display size.

The balance · restraint, made visible

Most of every page is Porcelain

This is the share of surface a typical layout gives each background. Porcelain dominates; Sand and Green are occasional; the accents are barely a sliver. Carbon is not on the bar — it is ink, layered over everything as type, never a surface of its own. If a layout feels loud, a colour has taken more than its share.

04 · TypographyLocked

Domine leads. Montserrat carries.

Two typefaces, one job each. A warm serif for what matters; a clean sans for everything that carries it. Type is always Carbon — hierarchy comes from size and weight, not colour.

Domine · Display & headlines
Skin is a relationship to be tended.
Ancient wisdom for modern people
A warm, sturdy serif with real presence at size. Weights 400 · 500 · 600 · 700.
Montserrat · Body & interface

Montserrat carries everything Domine does not need to say out loud — body copy, labels, navigation and data. Clean and geometric, quiet enough to let the serif lead, and legible down to the smallest dashboard figure.

  • DisplayDomine · 46–80px
  • HeadlineDomine · 26–36px
  • Sub-headDomine · 18–21px
  • BodyMontserrat · 15–16px
  • Small / labelMontserrat · 11–13px
05 · Spacing & shapeProposed

A rhythm everything sits on

A proposed 4-point spacing scale and a small set of corner radii. One rhythm, used everywhere, is what makes a page feel unhurried instead of crowded.

Spacing scale — multiples of 4
4  xs · hairline gaps
8  sm · within a component
12  md · label to value
16  lg · default gap between cards
24  xl · card padding
32  2xl · block separation
48  3xl · section breathing room
64  4xl · top and tail of a section
Corner radius
6 · pills, tags
12 · buttons, inputs
16 · cards, panels
100 · badges
Layout

Generous, never tight

Content holds to a ~1080px column with wide gutters. White space is a feature — the layouts breathe, nothing is crowded.

Soft edges, hairline borders

White cards on Porcelain, separated by a 1px warm line rather than heavy shadow. Lift only on hover.
