    How to read this page

    Windows. "Prior 90d" is the 90 days ending the day before the selected window starts, so the comparison never includes the window itself. Per-day figures use completed days only; today is excluded from rates and projections. A move of 15% or more against the prior 90 days is treated as material and carries an amber mark.

    Sources. Meta figures are Meta-reported (7-day click, 1-day view) from the Marketing API, synced to the warehouse. Shopify figures come from the daily overview table. Blended ROAS is Shopify total sales divided by Meta plus Google spend, the same formula as the Shopify dashboard; 2.20 is the monthly target. New-customer CAC is all paid spend divided by Shopify first-order count; new-customer ROAS is Shopify first-order revenue divided by all paid spend.

    What moved ROAS. ROAS = (1000 ÷ CPM) × CTR × click-to-purchase rate × order value. Each driver's share of the change is a log split, so the four bars add up to the whole move exactly and the order does not matter.

    Spend vs efficiency. The curve is a least-squares fit of daily ROAS on log daily spend across the prior 90 days and this window (days under £50 excluded). Marginal ROAS is what the fitted curve says the next £1 returns at the current daily spend. It reads a pattern across days, not a controlled test, and it uses Meta-reported revenue.

    Campaigns. Each campaign is judged against its own prior-90-day ROAS, never against the blended target. Prospecting and Offers are new-customer campaigns and run lower platform ROAS than Existing by design. The trend is a 7-day rolling ROAS line over the window. Budget, 7d is spend per day over the last 7 completed days against the campaign's current daily budget (the budget is the one set now, so recent days are the fair comparison). Ad and creative detail lives in Creative Analytics.

    Frequency. Daily frequency is impressions divided by people reached that day, so it is comparable across windows of any length. Top-3 share is the three biggest spenders' share of the window's ad spend.
